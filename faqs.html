Degree Planning & Focus Areas
Do I need to declare an Applied Physics concentration or focus area?

Yes. Students interested in pursuing an Applied Physics concentration or focus area should complete the Applied Physics Concentration/Focus Area Declaration (for unofficial tracking purposes only) when beginning to plan their coursework.

Students pursuing a concentration must also formally declare their concentration using the Stellic Degree Audit Tool.

Review the Degree Paths page to explore the available focus areas, course maps, and related coursework.

Can I change my concentration/focus area later?

Yes. If you are considering changing your focus area, work directly with your academic advisor to review your degree progress and remaining requirements.

Your advisor can help determine how a change in concentration/focus area may affect your planned coursework and ensure that any necessary adjustments support your academic goals and anticipated graduation timeline.

How do I know which courses count toward my concentration/focus area?

Start by reviewing the Degree Paths page and the course map provided for your selected concentrationfocus area. These resources can help you identify courses that align with your interests and plan your path through the program.

Work with your academic advisor to develop a course plan that supports your academic and professional goals while keeping you on track for graduation. Be sure to verify the official Degree Requirements applicable to your catalog year when making course selections.

How many courses are required for the master's degree?

Review the current Degree Requirements page for the official requirements applicable to your catalog year.

Is 615.641 Mathematical Methods for Physics and Engineering required in the first semester?

615.641 Mathematical Methods for Physics and Engineering is a common prerequisite for many Applied Physics courses and is commonly used in early degree planning, but students should follow their individual degree plan and advising guidance that applies to their situation.

Review the Degree Requirements page and your degree audit to confirm what is required for your program.

Courses & Registration
How do I register for courses?

Check the Academic Calendar for registration dates, then register through SIS.

Where can I see which courses are offered in an upcoming semester?

Use the Course Offering Schedule to plan your coursework across future semesters.

When preparing to register, use the EP Courses page to view current course offerings, schedules, and available sections. Course offerings are subject to change, so be sure to review the EP Courses page when planning your registration for an upcoming semester.

Is the Course Offering Schedule guaranteed?

No. The Course Offering Schedule is intended as a planning resource and may change based on instructor availability and program scheduling priorities.

Be sure to review the EP Courses page for current offerings when preparing to register.

What should I do if a course I want is full?

If a course is full, register for the waitlist. If a seat becomes available, you will receive a notification with instructions to accept the available spot.

Joining the waitlist is also important for course planning. Waitlist demand helps the program assess student interest and determine whether an additional section may be needed and can be offered that semester. For this reason, students who are interested in a full course are encouraged to join the waitlist rather than simply checking back for an opening.

What if SIS will not let me register?

For enrollment or account-management issues, use Student Case Management (SEAM).

Can I take courses outside of Applied Physics?

Yes. The Applied Physics Degree Requirements allow students to take courses outside of the Applied Physics program as part of their elective coursework.

Before registering for a course outside of Applied Physics, consult with your academic advisor to confirm that the course will apply appropriately toward your degree requirements and overall course plan.

Independent Study / Research
Can I complete an independent study or research project as part of my master's degree?

Yes. Students have the option to pursue an Applied Physics Project (615.800) or Directed Studies in Applied Physics (615.802), which count toward the degree credit requirement.

Key requirements include:

  • Prior approval is required. Students must submit an independent study/project proposal and obtain approval from their academic advisor and the Applied Physics program before enrolling in an independent study or research course.
  • An approved research advisor is required. The research advisor must be a current instructor in the Engineering for Professionals Applied Physics program.

Students interested in the thesis option should begin by discussing their plans with their academic advisor to ensure the thesis pathway can be appropriately incorporated into their degree plan.
